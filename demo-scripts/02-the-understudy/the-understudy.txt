EXT. THE ALBION THEATRE - NIGHT

Manchester, 1931. Rain on a playbill under glass: THE WINTER
WIDOW, and beneath it, in smaller type, MISS EVELYN
ALDRIDGE.

A queue of wet umbrellas goes round the corner.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - NIGHT

A row of gas footlights along the lip of the stage, each one
hooded in brass, each one burning.

Behind them, a painted parlour. A woman in an emerald gown
crosses to a window that opens onto nothing.

ANGLE ON - THE FOOTLIGHTS

One hood hangs loose on its bracket. The flame inside leans.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - PROMPT CORNER - NIGHT

Stage left, half a metre of floor and a shelf. VERA COYNE,
20s, holds the prompt book open with her thumb, mouthing
every line a half-beat ahead of the actress.

She does not look at the page. She has not needed to for a
month.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - WINGS - CONTINUOUS

STAGE MANAGER HOLLOWAY, 40s, clipboard, checks a watch
against nothing.

                      HOLLOWAY
                (low)
          Where's her change?

                      VERA (O.S.)
          Second interval.

                      HOLLOWAY
          She's got ninety seconds.

                      VERA (O.S.)
          She's had ninety seconds for four
          hundred performances.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - DRESSING ROOM ONE - NIGHT

Mirror bulbs, half of them out. EVELYN ALDRIDGE, 40s, holds
herself very straight while a dresser works the hooks up the
back of the emerald gown.

The gown is beaded to the shoulder. It weighs what a small
child weighs.

                      EVELYN
          Tighter.

                      DRESSER
          It's at the last hook, Miss
          Aldridge.

                      EVELYN
          Then move the hook.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - CORRIDOR - CONTINUOUS

Vera passes the door with the prompt book under her arm.
Slows. Doesn't stop.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - NIGHT

Evelyn, in the emerald gown, delivers the curtain line to a
house that has already begun to gather its coats.

                                                            CUT TO:

INT. ALBION THEATRE - PROMPT CORNER - NIGHT

Vera closes the prompt book. Rules a line under the date.

INT. PUBLIC HOUSE - NIGHT

Low ceiling. Company members crowded round two tables. Vera
on the end of a bench, half in her coat.

DESMOND CARR, 30s, leading man, drops onto the bench beside
her.

                      DESMOND
          You were three words ahead of her
          all night.

VERA                 Four, in the second act.
DESMOND                 You were four ahead in the second
act.

                      VERA
          Four, in the second act.

                      DESMOND
          You were four ahead in the second
          act.

They both stop. Laugh.

INT. PUBLIC HOUSE - LATER

                      DESMOND
          Ask Holloway for the Thursday
          matinee.

                      VERA
          Holloway doesn't give matinees.
          Holloway gives notes.

                      DESMOND
          Ask anyway.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - HOLLOWAY'S OFFICE - DAY

A desk, a safe, a schedule board. Holloway does not offer
the chair.

                      HOLLOWAY
          No.

                      VERA
          I know the whole of it.

                      HOLLOWAY
          Everyone knows the whole of it.
          That's what a prompt book is for.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - HOLLOWAY'S OFFICE - CONTINUOUS

Vera puts the prompt book on the desk. Doesn't let go of it
yet.

                      VERA
          Then take it off me and give me the
          Thursday.

Holloway looks at her hand on the book.

                      HOLLOWAY
          Leave it there.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - CORRIDOR - DAY

Vera walks out with nothing under her arm. Her hand keeps
closing on air.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - NIGHT

Evelyn, in the emerald gown, misses a cue.

The pause runs two seconds long. Three.

                      VERA (O.S.)
                (from the prompt corner,
                barely audible)
          The window, Aunt Beatrice.

Evelyn crosses to the window.

MONTAGE - EIGHT PERFORMANCES

Evelyn late on a cue. Vera's mouth moving in the dark. The
emerald gown going up on its hanger, coming down off its
hanger, going up again.

The hood on the footlight, still loose.

END MONTAGE

INT. ALBION THEATRE - DRESSING ROOM ONE - NIGHT

Evelyn, out of the gown, in a robe, works cold cream across
her face with both hands.

Her hands are not steady. She watches them until they are.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - WINGS - NIGHT

Desmond, in costume, waiting on his entrance. Vera beside
him.

                      DESMOND
          She's ill.

                      VERA
          She's tired.

                      DESMOND
          Those are the same word in this
          building.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - NIGHT

CLOSE ON - the loose hood. The flame leans against the brass
and blackens it.

A stagehand walks past it for the ninth night running.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - HOLLOWAY'S OFFICE - DAY

Holloway, on the telephone, writing nothing down.

                      HOLLOWAY
                (into phone)
          Then send whoever the agency has.
          Tonight.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - GREEN ROOM - DAY

The company reads a notice pinned to the board. Nobody
speaks.

Vera reads it twice.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - DRESSING ROOM TWO - NIGHT

Smaller mirror. Fewer bulbs. The emerald gown hangs on the
back of the door, let out two inches at the waist.

Vera looks at it the way you look at a dog that might not
know you.

                                                            DISSOLVE TO:

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - NIGHT

Vera, in the emerald gown, in the parlour that opens onto
nothing.

She does not miss the cue.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - WINGS - CONTINUOUS

Holloway watches from the dark with the clipboard against
his chest, not writing.

INT. PUBLIC HOUSE - NIGHT

The company, louder than usual. Vera in the middle of it for
the first time.

                      DESMOND
          To Thursday.

                      COMPANY
          To Thursday.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - CORRIDOR - NIGHT

Evelyn, in a coat, walking out past the notice board. She
does not read it.

She passes Vera in the corridor. Neither says anything.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - PROMPT CORNER - NIGHT

A YOUNG ASM, 19, holds the prompt book badly, thumb in the
wrong place.

She loses her line. Finds it. Loses it again.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - NIGHT

Vera covers the silence with a piece of business the play
does not contain.

The house never knows.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - HOLLOWAY'S OFFICE - DAY

                      HOLLOWAY
          You added four lines.

                      VERA
          I added six.

                      HOLLOWAY
          Don't do it again.

A beat.

                      HOLLOWAY (CONT'D)
          Do it again on Saturday if she
          drops it again.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - NIGHT

The emerald gown under the footlights. Vera turns downstage.

Behind her, the loose hood tips off its bracket and hangs by
one screw.

Nobody in the house looks at it.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - WINGS - CONTINUOUS

Holloway sees it. Starts forward. The scene is still
running.

He stops.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - NIGHT

The hood drops. The flame goes sideways into the painted
parlour flat.

Canvas takes light the way paper does.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - CONTINUOUS

Vera turns into it. The beaded hem of the emerald gown goes
up first.

She gets two steps before Desmond hits her at the waist and
takes her down out of frame.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - AUDITORIUM - CONTINUOUS

Four hundred people stand at once. The house lights come up
on a room already full of smoke.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - CONTINUOUS

Desmond rolls Vera in the safety curtain's weighted skirt.

The gown is gone from the knee down. Her right forearm,
where she put it out, is burned from wrist to elbow.

She is not making any sound at all.

                                                            FADE TO:

INT. HOSPITAL WARD - DAY

Rows of beds. Vera's right forearm dressed from wrist to
elbow.

Desmond, in his own clothes for once, on a chair too small
for him.

                      DESMOND
          Holloway's saying the bracket was
          sound.

                      VERA
          The bracket was loose in September.

                      DESMOND
          I know.

                      VERA
          You walked past it too.

INT. HOSPITAL WARD - LATER

Vera alone. She works the fingers of the right hand, one at
a time, and gets three of them.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - STAGE - DAY

Daylight through the scene dock doors. The parlour flat is a
black frame with nothing in it.

Vera walks the stage with her sleeve buttoned to the wrist
over the dressing.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - HOLLOWAY'S OFFICE - DAY

The schedule board is wiped. The safe is open and empty.

                      HOLLOWAY
          The company's dissolved as of
          Friday.

                      VERA
          And the inquiry?

                      HOLLOWAY
          There's no inquiry. There's an
          insurer.

INT. ALBION THEATRE - PROMPT CORNER - DAY

The prompt book on the shelf, cover scorched along one edge.

Vera picks it up left-handed.

EXT. THE ALBION THEATRE - DAY

The playbill case is empty. A bill-poster works a paste
brush over the glass.

INT. REPERTORY THEATRE, LEEDS - AUDITION ROOM - DAY

A trestle table. Three people behind it. Vera in front of
it, sleeve buttoned to the wrist.

                      CASTING WOMAN
          And you've played the Widow.

                      VERA
          Eleven times.

                      CASTING WOMAN
          For Aldridge.

                      VERA
          After her.

The casting woman looks at the sleeve. Vera unbuttons it and
puts the arm on the table.

                      VERA (CONT'D)
          Do you want the second act or the
          third?

FADE OUT.
