EXT. HARBOUR ROAD - NIGHT

Rain. A chain-link fence runs the length of the frame.
Beyond it, stacked containers go up four high, orange and
rust.

A sodium lamp buzzes over the gate. Everything under it is
the colour of weak tea.

EXT. DEPOT GATE - CONTINUOUS

A guard hut, lit from inside. TOMAS ADEYEMI, 50s, reads a
paperback with the spine broken back on itself.

A car horn. Two short taps.

                      TOMAS
                (not looking up)
          Badge.

INT. DISPATCH OFFICE - NIGHT

Strip lighting. Six monitors, four of them dead. MARA
OKONJO, 30s, in a grey parka she has not taken off in nine
hours, works a keyboard with two fingers.

The parka is soaked at the shoulders. She has not noticed.

A radio on the desk crackles.

                      VOICE ON RADIO (V.O.)
          Dispatch, this is Seventeen. I'm at
          the weighbridge and the weighbridge
          says I'm empty.

                      MARA
          Are you empty?

                      VOICE ON RADIO (V.O.)
          I am extremely not empty.

                      MARA
          Then the weighbridge is lying. Come
          round.

INT. DISPATCH OFFICE - CONTINUOUS

Mara pulls a clipboard off a nail. Runs a finger down the
manifest.

She stops. Goes back up. Down again.

                      MARA
          Huh.

EXT. WEIGHBRIDGE - NIGHT

A twelve-metre trailer rests on the plate. The display reads
0.00 T in red.

DEV RAMIREZ, 40s, steps down from the cab, hood up against
the rain.

                      DEV
          Nine times out of ten it's the load
          cell.

                      MARA (O.S.)
          And the tenth time?

                      DEV
          Somebody's been at it.

INT. DISPATCH OFFICE - NIGHT

CALLIE VOSS, 50s, night supervisor, comes in shaking water
off an umbrella she did not need.

                      CALLIE
          Log it as a fault. Engineering
          comes Thursday.

                      MARA
          It's Monday.

                      CALLIE
          Then log it as a fault and go home
          on time for once.

EXT. WEIGHBRIDGE - NIGHT

Dev crouches at the edge of the plate. Runs his hand along
the seam.

His hand comes back wet and black.

                      DEV
          That's not rain.

INT. DEPOT CANTEEN - NIGHT

Vending machine light. Mara sets down two coffees.

Dev wipes his hands on a paper towel. Across the back of his
right hand, a raised scar runs from the knuckle of his index
finger to the wrist. Old. Silvered.

                      MARA
          That from here?

                      DEV
                (covering it)
          Rigging accident. Different yard.

                      MARA
          Which yard?

                      DEV
          One that doesn't exist anymore.

INT. DISPATCH OFFICE - NIGHT

Mara, still in the grey parka, spreads three manifests
across the desk.

Three trailers. Three weights. Three signatures, all the
same hand.

                      MARA
                (to herself)
          Same pen, even.

EXT. CONTAINER STACK - NIGHT

Torchlight between the rows. Mara walks the aisle, counting
doors.

She reaches container 4-4-1. The seal is intact. She
photographs it.

EXT. CONTAINER STACK - CONTINUOUS

She walks two rows over. Container 4-4-2.

The seal is intact here too. But the number stamped in the
lead is the same number as the last one.

                      MARA
          That's not possible.

INT. GUARD HUT - NIGHT

Tomas turns a page.

                      MARA (O.S.)
          Who signed for the four-forties?

                      TOMAS
          Gate log's on the hook.

The hook is empty.

EXT. DEPOT YARD - NIGHT

Mara crosses the open yard, head down, rain running off the
parka hood.

Behind her, in the dark, a container door swings a hand's
width and stops.

EXT. LOADING DOCK - NIGHT

Rain sheets off the awning. Two pallet jacks, one dead
forklift, the blue sedan idles by the gate, wipers going.

Under the sodium wash the whole dock is one colour.

Mara comes down the steps and gets in the car.

INT. BLUE SEDAN - CONTINUOUS

She doesn't start driving. Watches the dock through the
wipers.

The container door swings again. Stops again.

INT. BLUE SEDAN - LATER

Mara's phone, face up on the passenger seat. A photo of the
duplicate seal.

She types a message. Deletes it. Types it again.

EXT. HARBOUR ROAD - NIGHT

Headlights sweep past the fence and keep going.

INT. MARA'S FLAT - NIGHT

Small. Clean. She hangs the grey parka on the back of the
door, where it drips onto newspaper laid down for that
purpose.

INT. MARA'S FLAT - CONTINUOUS

She spreads the photographs on the kitchen table. Four
seals. Two numbers.

INT. DISPATCH OFFICE - DAY

Daylight makes the office worse. Callie has the fault log
open.

                      CALLIE
          You went into the stack.

                      MARA
          I counted doors.

                      CALLIE
          You went into the stack at two in
          the morning on your own.

Behind them, through the window, the dead forklift stands
where it stood last night.

                      MARA
          Who has seal stock?

                      CALLIE
          Nobody has seal stock. Seal stock
          is sealed.

INT. RECORDS ROOM - DAY

Box files to the ceiling. Mara pulls 2024, Q3.

Every gate log for eleven weeks is missing its Tuesday.

EXT. DEPOT CAR PARK - DAY

Mara crosses to the blue sedan, keys out, parka over one
arm.

Dev is leaning on the wing.

                      DEV
          You photographed a seal.

                      MARA
          I photographed four.

                      DEV
          Put them somewhere that isn't your
          phone.

INT. BLUE SEDAN - CONTINUOUS

Dev gets in the passenger side without being asked. Water
off his jacket onto the seat.

                      DEV
          Drive somewhere.

                      MARA
          Where?

                      DEV
          Anywhere with a camera on it.

EXT. PETROL STATION - DAY

Fluorescent under the canopy. Four cameras. Mara parks under
all of them.

INT. BLUE SEDAN - CONTINUOUS

                      DEV
          Eleven weeks of Tuesdays.

                      MARA
          You knew.

                      DEV
          I knew a number was wrong. I didn't
          know which number.

INT. PETROL STATION SHOP - DAY

Dev pays for two coffees. The scar on his hand catches the
light as he counts change.

The CASHIER, 20s, looks at it a half-second too long.

                      DEV
                (under his breath)
          Yeah.

INT. DISPATCH OFFICE - NIGHT

Mara logs in. Her access to the gate system returns a red
banner.

PERMISSIONS UPDATED. CONTACT YOUR SUPERVISOR.

INT. CALLIE'S OFFICE - NIGHT

                      CALLIE
          It's not me.

                      MARA
          It says supervisor.

                      CALLIE
          It says supervisor because the
          system only knows two words.

Callie looks at the door. Closes it.

                      CALLIE (CONT'D)
          Go home, Mara.

EXT. DEPOT YARD - NIGHT

Mara walks out. Tomas watches her go from the hut and does
not read his book.

INT. GUARD HUT - CONTINUOUS

Tomas picks up a phone that is not his phone.

EXT. LOADING DOCK - NIGHT

The blue sedan waits by the gate with the engine off and the
lights off.

Mara, inside it, watches a container door open properly this
time.

Two men she does not recognise walk a pallet jack down the
ramp.

EXT. CONTAINER STACK - NIGHT

Torchlight. Different torch. Two of them.

EXT. LOADING DOCK - NIGHT

The pallet jack goes up the ramp of a trailer with no
plates.

INT. BLUE SEDAN - CONTINUOUS

Mara films it through the windscreen. Her hands are steady.
Her breathing is not.

INT. MARA'S FLAT - NIGHT

The door is open four inches. She did not leave it open four
inches.

INT. MARA'S FLAT - CONTINUOUS

Nothing is broken. Nothing is missing. The photographs on
the kitchen table have been squared into a neat stack.

That is worse.

EXT. HARBOUR ROAD - DAWN

Mara walks the length of the fence in the grey parka, phone
to her ear.

                      MARA
                (into phone)
          Then who does investigate it?

INT. DEV'S KITCHEN - DAY

Dev, no jacket, pours coffee.

                      DEV
          Port authority takes it if there's
          a customs number attached. There's
          never a customs number attached.
          That's the design.

                      MARA
          So it's nobody.

                      DEV
          It's nobody until it's a body.

INT. DEV'S KITCHEN - CONTINUOUS

Mara sets her phone on the counter. Plays the footage.

Dev watches it twice.

                      DEV
          The one on the left is the one who
          signs.

INT. RECORDS ROOM - DAY

Mara pulls 2024, Q4. Tuesdays intact.

She checks the shelf above. 2025, Q1. Tuesdays gone.

She looks up. On the top shelf, a box that is not a box
file: seal stock, unopened, forty units.

EXT. DEPOT YARD - NIGHT

Rain again. Mara crosses the yard with the seal box under
the parka.

Headlights swing into the gate. She drops behind the dead
forklift.

A trailer with no plates rolls past two metres away.

EXT. DEPOT YARD - CONTINUOUS

The trailer stops. A door opens. Boots on wet concrete.

Mara does not move.

                      TOMAS (O.S.)
          She's not out here.

A long beat.

                      TOMAS (O.S.) (CONT'D)
          I said she's not out here.

The boots go back. The door shuts.

EXT. DEPOT GATE - NIGHT

Tomas raises the barrier for the plateless trailer. Watches
it go. Then lowers the barrier and walks back to the hut
without looking at the yard.

EXT. LOADING DOCK - NIGHT

Mara comes out from behind the forklift with the box.

The blue sedan is where she left it. Dev is standing beside
it, hood down, rain on his face.

He puts out his right hand for the box. The scar shows.

                      DEV
          Give it here.

Mara looks at the hand. Does not move.

                      MARA
          Which yard, Dev?

Off Dev, not answering.

FADE OUT.
